LOCATION STUDY · TORONTO RESALE & SUPPLY · DATA AS OF 2026-08-19

One block, read hard:
the corridor after the crash

What a single North Toronto transit node — its podium, its slab, its courtyard — says about the market around it.

In July 2026 the GTA resale market tightened for a third straight month: sales held flat while new listings fell 17.8 per cent, the steepest withdrawal of supply all year. Prices are still below last year, but the decline is losing momentum. Behind the resale tape, the construction pipeline has collapsed to a 1996 low. The closest historical analogue is the post-2017 digestion — but this cycle, with a supply cliff behind it, has no true precedent.

§0 · Key findings

Tightening without a recovery — yet

The market is absorbing inventory faster than it is being replenished; prices follow balance with a lag, and balance turned first.

Three numbers carry this report. First, supply: new listings fell to 14,484 in July 2026, down 17.8 per cent from a year earlier — the largest annual decline of 2026 — while sales held essentially flat at 5,995. Second, balance: the sales-to-new-listings ratio reached 41.4 per cent, up from 34.6 per cent last July, the third straight monthly tightening. Third, price: the average sale price of $1,003,956 is still 4.5 per cent below a year ago, but the annual decline has narrowed every month since May.

STREET-LEVEL MIXED USE

The retail strip is the node's amenity signal — and its zoning tell.

Corridor planning in Toronto privileges mixed-use mid-rise along arterials. A grocery-anchored podium at grade with residential above is the form the Official Plan steers toward; sites assembled along these corridors trade on that permission. The podium is also the consumer-facing evidence that the node is a walkable daily-needs cluster, the attribute that keeps both rents and resale liquidity resilient through downcycles.

THE SELLABLE FLOOR PLATES

Mid-rise condominium stock — the segment carrying the deepest price declines.

Condo apartments averaged $636,323 across the GTA in July 2026, and $672,807 in the City of Toronto; the segment was down 9.5 per cent year-over-year in June, the weakest major home type. It is also the segment where the construction pipeline has collapsed hardest — GTHA condo starts fell 79 per cent in Q1-2025 — setting up a thin delivery slate by 2027–28.

GARDEN-COURT RENTAL STOCK

Post-war rental blocks — the quiet absorber of the region's housing demand.

Garden-court apartments are the corridor's original intensification. Their economics moved in 2025: GTA purpose-built vacancy reached 3.0 per cent, the first time at that level since the pandemic, as student and newcomer inflows slowed and condo owners shifted units into rental. Average two-bedroom purpose-built rent still grew 3.5 per cent to $2,034 — pressure beneath the softening.

§2 · The market

Nine years of corridor history in one wall chart

Four regimes since 2017: a policy break, a pandemic boom, a rate shock, and a tariff-era trough that is now tightening.

The closest historical analogue to the present window is the 2018–19 digestion after the Fair Housing Plan — a demand shock worked off through two quiet years while supply stayed short. This cycle rhymes with it, but adds something 2018 never had: a construction pipeline that has already collapsed. 2025 closed with 62,433 sales, the lowest annual total in the series, and an average price of $1,067,968, down 4.7 per cent.

With sales accounting for a larger share of listings, buyers may find there is less room to negotiate moving forward. If current trends continue, home prices could start to level off compared to last year.

— DANIEL STEINFELD, TRREB PRESIDENT, 2026-08-06

Context July 2026 release, after new listings fell 17.8 per cent year-over-year. Why it matters The board's own language shifted from defending affordability to flagging tightening — the official marker of the balance turn.

The rental release valve. The ownership market's correction has been cushioned by a rental market that finally has slack: GTA purpose-built vacancy hit 3.0 per cent in 2025 (condo rental still tight at 1.0 per cent), and the average two-bedroom turnover rent declined to $2,547 from $2,612. Pressure remains at the bottom of the rent distribution — the cheapest quartile still posts near-1-per-cent vacancy — so the relief is real but regressive in shape. §3 · The supply cliff

What is not being built

Completions crest this year at a record; starts have already fallen off the cliff. The gap between the two is the next cycle's story.

As of Q1-2025, 69,042 condominium units were under construction across the GTHA — down a third in two years — and completions are projected to peak at 31,396 units in 2025 before falling to 17,487 in 2026. Starts tell the forward story: 497 condo units broke ground in Q1-2025, 79 per cent below a year earlier and 88 per cent below the ten-year average — the lowest quarterly reading since 1996. With roughly 70 per cent pre-construction sales required to finance a tower, record-low presales translate directly into missing supply two to three years out.

The new condo market is currently working through its most challenging period to date… The magnitude of this slowdown will result in severe supply repercussions.

— SHAUN HILDEBRAND, PRESIDENT, URBANATION, 2025-04-15

Context Q1-2025 survey: starts at a 1996 low, unsold inventory at a record 24,045 units (Q2-2025). Why it matters The pipeline math in the chart above is not a projection of preference — it is already financed, or already not.

§4 · The verdict

Stabilizing — not yet a recovery

The evidence weighs toward a market finding its floor. Two triggers would upgrade the reading; three falsifiers would break it.

For the corridor node this report started from, the implication is direct: the resale tape is tightening first, the price tape follows with a lag, and the supply cliff behind both argues that the next shortage is being built right now — by omission. What would prove stabilization real is printed prices; what would break it is a renewed flood of listings.
